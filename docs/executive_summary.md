# Executive Summary: Retail Performance Review

**To:** COO | **From:** Dolfine Ngare, Data Analyst | **Date:** October 2026
**Period:** Dec 2010 – 9 Dec 2011 | **Data:** 534,131 cleaned transactions

## Bottom line
The business is busier because of the Christmas season, not because something is wrong. Cancellations are under control, but 20 big customers have stopped buying and should be contacted.

## Key findings
1. **Growth is seasonal.** Orders more than doubled from January to November (+137%). Sep–Nov alone brought in 35% of the year's £10.2M gross sales.
2. **Order size is stable.** The average order stayed about the same (£436 → £453, +4%). Customers are not placing smaller orders.
3. **Cancellations are not out of control.** They run at 3–5% of sales, below the 5% limit. The two spikes (January and December) were single huge orders cancelled within minutes, most likely typing errors.
4. **A small group of customers matters most.** The top 10% of customers bring in 60% of identified revenue. 20 of them have not ordered in 90+ days (about £157K of past revenue). The Netherlands and Ireland accounts are still active.

## Recommendations
| # | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contact the 20 quiet big customers, starting with long-term loyal buyers | Sales team |
| 2 | Add an order-system alert for unusually large quantities | Operations / IT |
| 3 | Plan stock and staffing for the Sep–Nov peak | Operations |
| 4 | Encourage guest buyers to create accounts (15% of revenue is from unidentified buyers) | Marketing |

## What we could not measure
- **Profit:** no cost data. Finance would need to share product costs.
- **Why customers cancel or stop buying:** the data shows who and when, not why.
- **Year-on-year growth:** data starts in Dec 2010, so Sep–Nov cannot be compared with the previous year.

## Open questions
- April cancellations (6.5%) are above the limit and still being checked.
- Customer 15098 may be a refunded pricing error rather than a real big customer.
